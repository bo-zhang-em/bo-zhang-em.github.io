Bo Zhang

Antennas, metasurfaces
& generative design

I am a Research Fellow in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at the National University of Singapore (NUS). My research focuses on using metalines, metasurfaces and metamaterials to control antenna radiation through engineered electromagnetic boundary conditions.

I am interested in improving radiation-pattern roundness and wireless coverage in constrained environments. My current work also explores generative machine learning for the inverse design of electromagnetic structures, including high-permeability metamaterials.

Near-field radiation control

Compact near-field metasurfaces for beam tilting

I investigate compact metasurfaces placed close to antennas to shape their radiation patterns and enable two-dimensional beam tilting. My ICEAA 2026 contribution explores cWGAN-synthesized unit cells for this application.

Metastructures and antenna integration

Radiation control on finite ground planes

This work uses henge-like metarings to localize monopole radiation and suppress ground-current scattering. A related research direction studies anisotropic metamaterial covers for suppressing guided waves and improving radiation-pattern roundness.

Current research

Generative design of electromagnetic structures

I explore GAN-based inverse design for electromagnetic structures, including broadband high-permeability metamaterials. This research connects learned geometry generation with electromagnetic modeling and validation.
